Some situations need a vet or emergency animal hospital immediately rather than a schedule change:
trouble breathing, collapse, a seizure, heavy bleeding, a swollen hard belly with retching, suspected
poisoning, or being unable to urinate.

Common household toxins include chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol (in some gum and toothpaste),
onions and garlic, lilies (for cats), and many human medicines. If you think a pet ate something toxic,
call your vet or an animal poison control line right away; do not wait for symptoms.

Keep your vet's number and the nearest 24-hour emergency clinic's number somewhere easy to find.
